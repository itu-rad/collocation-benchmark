\title{\sysname: Collocation-Aware, End-to-End Benchmarking of
Machine-Learning Pipelines}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\affiliation{\institution{Anonymous Institution}\city{}\country{}}

\begin{abstract}
Standardized ML benchmarks measure isolated models on isolated accelerators:
a single feed-forward network, fed preprocessed tensors, timed at the device
boundary. Production ML systems look nothing like this. Agentic and
retrieval-augmented pipelines execute cyclic, input-dependent graphs; their
performance is set by data movement as much as by compute; and, especially on
resource-constrained nodes, they run \emph{collocated}, contending for shared
resources that no single-stream benchmark can observe. We present \sysname{},
a declarative framework that benchmarks ML \emph{pipelines} end to end:
arbitrary (cyclic) stage graphs defined in configuration, open- and
closed-loop load generation, per-stage and per-process instrumentation, and
first-class collocation of concurrent pipelines. Two instrument-fidelity
experiments bound \sysname{}'s own footprint --- per-stage dispatch is tens of
microseconds and flat in graph depth, and wrapping a real GPU training
workload costs $-0.05\%$ [$-0.29, +0.14$] per step, statistically
indistinguishable from zero. We then use \sysname{} to show what isolated benchmarks miss, with case
studies on two machines that make contention effects vivid --- unified-memory
nodes (a \SI{16}{\giga\byte} Apple M2~Pro and a \SI{128}{\giga\byte}
NVIDIA GB10), where every engine draws on one shared memory: a
staged contention study that descends, by single-line configuration edits,
from ``collocated pipelines interfere'' to a quantitative law ---
\pending{on our unified-memory testbeds, foreground decode throughput
degrades in proportion to co-runner \emph{memory traffic}, largely
independent of which engine generates it}; a configuration-only restructuring of an agentic RAG
pipeline that trades one large model against a graph of small specialists at
matched answer quality; and a demonstration that MLPerf Inference's four load
scenarios are special cases of \sysname{}'s configuration space. All
configurations, traces, and analysis scripts are released.
\end{abstract}
