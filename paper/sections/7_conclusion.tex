\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Benchmarks shape what systems builders optimize. Today's standardized suites,
built to compare accelerators, cannot represent the cyclic, end-to-end,
collocated workloads that now dominate ML deployments --- so those workloads'
bottlenecks go unmeasured and unoptimized. \sysname{} closes that gap with a
small set of composable ideas: stages in declarative graphs, pluggable load
schedules, process-granular collocation with per-pipeline attribution, and a
pre-specified measurement methodology in which every knob has a derivation
and every arrival process is verified from its own trace. We calibrated the instrument (per-stage dispatch flat in depth at
\SI{60}{}--\SI{85}{\micro\second}; modularity overhead indistinguishable
from zero; tracing a uniform, accounted $+2.1\%$), and we used it for studies a single-stream harness cannot express: a
staged collocation analysis that descends from symptom toward mechanism by
single-element configuration diffs, testing a stated bandwidth-pricing
hypothesis with falsification branches; a quality-bounded,
configuration-only topology study of agentic RAG with a capacity sweep to
each device's ceiling; and a load-pattern reduction locating MLPerf's
scenarios inside \sysname{}'s configuration space. Configurations, traces, and analysis
scripts will be released for artifact evaluation. Future work extends the same
method to discrete-memory servers and multi-node pipelines, where the
contended resources --- and therefore the laws --- will differ, but the
instrument need not.
