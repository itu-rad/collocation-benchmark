\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Benchmarks shape what systems builders optimize. Today's standardized suites,
built to compare accelerators, cannot represent the cyclic, end-to-end,
collocated workloads that now dominate ML deployments --- so those workloads'
bottlenecks go unmeasured and unoptimized. \sysname{} closes that gap with a
small set of composable ideas: stages in declarative graphs, pluggable load
schedules, process-granular collocation with per-pipeline attribution, and a
pre-registered measurement methodology in which every knob has a derivation
and every arrival process is verified from its own trace. We showed the
instrument stays out of its measurements (per-stage dispatch flat at tens of
microseconds; wrapping real training statistically indistinguishable from
zero), and we used it for studies a single-stream harness cannot express: a
staged collocation analysis that descends from symptom to a mechanism priced
in memory traffic, a quality-conditioned, configuration-only topology
trade-off for agentic RAG with a capacity sweep to each device's ceiling, and
a load-pattern reduction showing MLPerf's scenarios are points in
\sysname{}'s configuration space. Configurations, traces, and analysis
scripts are released for artifact evaluation. Future work extends the same
method to discrete-memory servers and multi-node pipelines, where the
contended resources --- and therefore the laws --- will differ, but the
instrument need not.
