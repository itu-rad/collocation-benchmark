\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Standardized benchmarks.} MLPerf~\cite{reddi2020mlperf} anchors
ML performance measurement; its closed division fixes architectures and
weights so hardware can be compared fairly. The structure this requires
creates gaps for systems research: segmentation by scale
(\emph{Tiny}~\cite{banbury2021mlperftiny}, \emph{Edge}, \emph{Datacenter})
leaves no continuous scalability axis, and the isolated single-model regime
excludes the behaviors of \S\ref{sec:intro}. The wider ecosystem is
fragmented --- EEMBC MLMark (embedded, largely pre-transformer), AI
Benchmark~\cite{ignatov2019aibenchmark} (mobile SoC scorecard),
TPCx-AI~\cite{brucke2023tpcxai} (end-to-end but enterprise-scale and
classical-ML-heavy), and AISBench~\cite{aisbench}, whose end-to-end
multi-stream diagnosis we share but which targets large servers and offers
neither declarative graph reconfiguration nor per-process collocation
attribution.

\paragraph{Interference characterization.} A substantial literature induces
and models workload interference: Bubble-Up~\cite{mars2011bubbleup} and
Bubble-Flux~\cite{yang2013bubbleflux} price sensitivity to a synthetic
memory-pressure bubble; Paragon and Quasar~\cite{delimitrou2013paragon,
delimitrou2014quasar} classify workloads for interference-aware scheduling;
Heracles~\cite{lo2015heracles} and PARTIES~\cite{chen2019parties} isolate
latency-critical services from best-effort co-runners;
MemGuard~\cite{yun2013memguard} regulates per-core memory bandwidth on
multicore SoCs. This line establishes that memory-system interference is
real and often bandwidth-priced --- on CPUs, with synthetic co-runners, and
usually in service of a scheduler. Our contention study differs in three
ways: the contenders are full ML \emph{pipelines} (and, in later stages,
single-resource co-runners spanning CPU, GPU, \emph{and} a neural
accelerator) on unified-memory platforms where all engines draw on one DRAM
budget; attribution is per-pipeline and, on Apple silicon, per-engine in
\emph{bytes}, from the memory controller's own counters; and the descent
from production-shaped symptom to mechanism is itself conducted through the
benchmark's configuration space --- a method, not only a measurement. What
the field learns is not ``bandwidth contention exists'' but where the
unified-memory instantiation of that law does and does not follow the
classical picture, measured with attribution the classical studies lacked.

\paragraph{Serving systems and LLM load tools.} Pipeline execution is not
new: Ray Serve~\cite{moritz2018ray}, Triton ensembles, and
InferLine~\cite{crankshaw2020inferline} compose model DAGs for production
serving, and LLM-era studies dissect serving performance --- notably the
prefill/decode phase split that Splitwise~\cite{patel2024splitwise},
Sarathi~\cite{agrawal2024sarathi}, and DistServe~\cite{zhong2024distserve}
exploit, which our Stage-D control leans on. Load tools such as
LLMPerf~\cite{llmperf} drive open-loop traffic at model endpoints. These are
the realistic counterfactual to \sysname{}: a researcher could lash a
serving framework to a load tool and a profiler. What that lashup lacks is
precisely what our experiments require: one declarative artifact in which
topology, placement, schedule, and collocation are all reconfigurable
\emph{measurement variables}; per-stage timing native to the execution
rather than sampled around it; arrival-trace verification; and cyclic graphs
(production servers deliberately reject cycles). \sysname{} is not a serving
system --- it trades throughput engineering for measurement fidelity, the
reverse of their trade.

\paragraph{The measurement boundary.} Preloading preprocessed samples
isolates the accelerator and blinds the harness to the stages that
end-to-end studies find dominant~\cite{coleman2017dawnbench}. \sysname{}
makes every stage separately timed and lets preloading be a per-stage
configuration choice, so the MLPerf-style control and the end-to-end
measurement are two configs of one artifact.

\paragraph{Benchmarks as research instruments.} Standardization and
exploration pull oppositely: fair comparison wants monolithic, frozen
implementations; research wants to swap a retriever, halve a model, add a
co-runner, and observe. In a monolithic harness each edit is invasive and
non-transferable. In \sysname{} it is a configuration diff --- and
\S\ref{sec:results-staged} elevates that property into an experimental
method, descending from system to mechanism one diff at a time.
