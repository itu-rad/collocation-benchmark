\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Machine learning now runs everywhere from laptop SoCs to rack-scale
accelerators, and benchmarking has rightly become the field's shared
instrument for progress. But the instrument was built for a workload shape
that is disappearing. Standardized suites such as MLPerf
Inference~\cite{reddi2020mlperf} measure a single, fixed, feed-forward model,
fed preloaded and preprocessed samples, timed at the accelerator boundary, in
isolation. Each of those design choices was correct for cross-vendor hardware
comparison --- and each one hides a class of behavior that now dominates real
deployments.

We identify three widening gaps. \textbf{First, execution is no longer
linear.} Agentic workflows and retrieval-augmented generation
(RAG)~\cite{lewis2020rag} execute input-dependent graphs: conditional
branches, fan-out to specialist models, and feedback cycles such as
retrieve--grade--rewrite loops~\cite{asai2024selfrag}. A benchmark that can
only express ``one forward pass'' cannot even \emph{represent} these
workloads, let alone locate their bottlenecks. \textbf{Second, the
measurement boundary is wrong.} By timing only the accelerator, standard
suites exclude data loading, preprocessing, retrieval, and inter-stage data
movement --- precisely the components that end-to-end studies repeatedly find
dominant --- and so reward peak-FLOPs designs over balanced systems.
\textbf{Third, isolation is a fiction.} On edge and workstation-class nodes,
inference services run beside fine-tuning jobs, index refreshes, and other
tenants. What resource they contend for depends on the platform ---
device time on discrete GPUs, shared memory bandwidth on unified-memory SoCs
--- but in every case the interference is invisible to a benchmark that
measures one workload at a time, and unattributable without per-workload
instrumentation.

This paper presents \sysname{}, a benchmarking framework built for these
three gaps rather than around them. \sysname{} decomposes a workload into
\emph{stages} --- data loading, preprocessing, model execution, retrieval,
routing --- composed into a directed graph, cycles included, entirely in
declarative configuration. A load generator drives the graph with open-loop
(Poisson, fixed-interval, saturating) or closed-loop schedules, and every run
emits a monotonic per-stage timing trace, a per-query answer record, and an
intended-versus-realized arrival log. Pipelines are isolated at the process
boundary and instrumented per process, so an arbitrary number of collocated
pipelines can be launched \emph{and attributed} concurrently. \sysname{}
\emph{measures and attributes} contention; it deliberately does not manage
it.

Because a benchmarking framework is itself a measurement instrument, we first
bound its footprint. Per-stage dispatch costs tens of microseconds, is flat in
graph depth, and passes payloads by reference at $O(1)$ cost
(\S\ref{sec:results-e1}); wrapping a real GPU fine-tuning workload in the full
graph-queue-thread structure changes per-step latency by $-0.05\%$
[$-0.29,+0.14$] --- statistically indistinguishable from zero
(\S\ref{sec:results-e2}). The instrument, in other words, stays out of its own
measurements.

We then use \sysname{} to produce three results that isolated,
single-stream benchmarks structurally cannot. As testbeds we choose two
single-node machines from opposite ends of the \emph{unified-memory} design
space --- a \SI{16}{\giga\byte} Apple M2~Pro and a \SI{128}{\giga\byte}
NVIDIA GB10 --- because shared-memory platforms make collocation effects
especially consequential and especially hard to attribute, exactly the regime
a collocation-aware instrument should prove itself in:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A staged contention study} (\S\ref{sec:results-staged}) that
  begins with a production-shaped pair --- a RAG serving pipeline collocated
  with corpus-indexing jobs --- and descends to mechanism by changing
  \emph{exactly one configuration element per step}: first the background's
  arrival schedule, then its stage list (pure-CPU, pure-GPU, and
  Neural-Engine co-runners), then the foreground itself (bare decode, with a
  prefill/decode split as a built-in control).
  \pending{The closing figure overlays the system-level degradation points on
  the mechanism-level dose--response curve: interference is priced in
  co-runner memory traffic, largely engine-independent, and concentrated in
  the bandwidth-bound decode phase.} The experiment doubles as a live
  demonstration of declarative reconfigurability: the paper shows the literal
  configuration diffs.
  \item \textbf{A topology study of agentic RAG} (\S\ref{sec:results-e4}):
  one large model versus a graph of small specialists, swapped by
  configuration alone, with size-matched and shared-instance controls that
  separate model size, physical residency, and graph structure --- every
  speed claim conditioned on exact-match/F1 answer quality.
  \pending{headline trade-off numbers.}
  \item \textbf{A reduction of MLPerf Inference} (\S\ref{sec:results-e5}):
  each of its four load scenarios is recovered as a \sysname{} load-generator
  configuration, verified from the arrival trace --- positioning \sysname{}
  as a superset of, not a competitor to, the standard methodology.
\end{itemize}

\noindent This paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We characterize why standardized benchmarks under-serve ML
  \emph{systems} research: linear-graph assumptions, accelerator-boundary
  timing, and the isolation fiction (\S\ref{sec:background}).
  \item We present the design of \sysname{}: declarative cyclic stage graphs,
  pluggable load schedules, and process-granular, collocation-aware
  instrumentation (\S\ref{sec:framework}) --- with measured evidence that the
  instrument's own footprint is negligible (\S\ref{sec:results-e1},
  \S\ref{sec:results-e2}).
  \item We introduce a pre-registered measurement methodology --- every
  configuration knob derived from a stated rule with pilot-measured inputs,
  hierarchical run-level statistics, and arrival-trace verification
  (\S\ref{sec:setup}) --- and release it with the artifact.
  \item We use \sysname{} for three case studies beyond the reach of
  single-stream suites: a staged collocation study that, on our
  unified-memory testbeds, prices interference in co-runner memory traffic;
  a configuration-only topology trade-off for agentic RAG, conditioned on
  answer quality; and a load-pattern reduction of MLPerf Inference
  (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}
