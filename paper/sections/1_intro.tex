\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Benchmarks are the ML systems community's shared instruments, and the
standardized suites --- MLPerf Inference~\cite{reddi2020mlperf} foremost ---
were engineered for one purpose: fair cross-vendor comparison of
accelerators. That purpose dictates a single fixed feed-forward model, fed
preloaded and preprocessed samples, timed at the device boundary, in
isolation. Each choice is the right control for hardware comparison, and
each hides a class of behavior that increasingly dominates deployments.

We identify three widening gaps. \textbf{First, execution is no longer
linear.} Agentic workflows and retrieval-augmented generation
(RAG)~\cite{lewis2020rag} execute input-dependent graphs. In a RAG pipeline,
a query retrieves candidate documents, a model \emph{grades} their relevance,
another generates an answer, a third checks that answer against the retrieved
evidence, and a failed check re-enters retrieval with a rewritten query
--- a bounded feedback \emph{cycle}, not a
pass~\cite{asai2024selfrag}. A harness that can only express one forward pass
cannot represent such workloads, let alone locate their bottlenecks.
\textbf{Second, the measurement boundary is too narrow.} Timing only the
accelerator excludes data loading, preprocessing, retrieval, and inter-stage
movement --- components that end-to-end studies repeatedly find
dominant~\cite{coleman2017dawnbench} --- and so rewards peak-FLOPs designs
over balanced systems. \textbf{Third, workloads are collocated.} Inference
services share nodes with fine-tuning jobs, index refreshes, and other
tenants. Which resource they contend for is platform-dependent --- device
time on discrete GPUs, shared memory bandwidth on unified-memory SoCs --- but
in every case the interference is invisible to a benchmark that measures one
workload at a time, and unattributable without per-workload instrumentation.

\sysname{} is a benchmarking framework built for these gaps. It decomposes a
workload into \emph{stages} composed into a directed graph --- cycles
included --- entirely in declarative configuration; a load generator drives
the graph with open-loop (Poisson, fixed-interval, saturating) or closed-loop
schedules; and every run emits a monotonic per-stage timing trace, a
per-query answer record, and a log of intended-versus-realized arrival times.
Pipelines are isolated at the OS-process boundary and instrumented per
process, so collocated pipelines are separately attributable. \sysname{}
\emph{measures and attributes} contention; it deliberately does not manage
it. Prior systems cover fragments of this space --- serving frameworks
express pipelines but do not measure them against a load model; interference
studies induce collocation but on fixed workload pairs; load generators
drive open-loop traffic at single models (\S\ref{sec:background}) --- and it
is the combination, in one reconfigurable instrument, that enables the
experiments below.

An instrument must first be calibrated. Bare per-stage dispatch costs
\SI{60}{}--\SI{85}{\micro\second}, flat in graph depth, with $O(1)$
pass-by-reference data movement (\S\ref{sec:results-fidelity}); wrapping a
real GPU fine-tune in the full graph-queue-thread structure measures
$-0.05\%$ [$-0.29,+0.14$] per step --- indistinguishable from zero --- and
the tracing layer, which \emph{is} the instrument and is enabled in every
case study, adds a uniform $+2.1\%$ whose effect on within-framework
comparisons we bound explicitly.

We then run three case studies. As testbeds we choose two machines from
opposite ends of the \emph{unified-memory} design space --- a
\SI{16}{\giga\byte} Apple M2~Pro and a \SI{128}{\giga\byte} NVIDIA GB10 ---
because shared-memory platforms concentrate collocation effects in a
resource no per-device counter attributes, exactly the regime a
collocation-aware instrument should prove itself in.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The contention study} (\S\ref{sec:results-staged}) starts
  from a production-shaped symptom --- RAG serving collocated with corpus
  indexing --- and descends toward mechanism in four stages, each changing
  \emph{exactly one} configuration element: the background's arrival
  schedule; its stage list (pure-CPU, pure-GPU, and Neural-Engine
  co-runners); then the foreground itself (bare LLM decode, with the
  prefill/decode split as a counter-free control). It tests a stated
  hypothesis --- degradation is priced in co-runner \emph{memory traffic},
  not co-runner identity --- with pre-specified falsification outcomes;
  \pending{measured outcome}. The descent itself doubles as a demonstration
  of declarative reconfigurability: the paper shows the literal diffs.
  \item \textbf{The topology study} (\S\ref{sec:results-topology}): one
  large model versus a graph of small specialists for agentic RAG, swapped
  by configuration alone, with size-matched and shared-instance controls
  separating model size, physical residency, and graph structure. Speed
  claims are reported with \emph{bounded} answer-quality differences, never
  bare. A capacity sweep along the model-size axis --- one configuration
  field --- runs each device to its residency ceiling.
  \item \textbf{The load-pattern reduction} (\S\ref{sec:results-reduction}):
  MLPerf Inference's four scenarios reproduced as scheduler configurations
  and verified from arrival traces --- scoped deliberately to the load
  patterns, not to certified-harness semantics.
\end{itemize}

\noindent We make three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The \sysname{} design} (\S\ref{sec:framework}): declarative
  cyclic stage graphs, pluggable load schedules, and process-granular
  collocation attribution --- with measured calibration showing the
  instrument's footprint is bounded and small
  (\S\ref{sec:results-fidelity}).
  \item \textbf{A measurement methodology} (\S\ref{sec:setup}): every
  configuration knob derived from a stated rule with pilot-measured inputs
  and committed before collection; run-level hierarchical statistics;
  arrival processes verified from their own traces; and hypotheses for
  pending measurements stated with falsification outcomes.
  \item \textbf{The staged-descent method and its case studies}
  (\S\ref{sec:results}): system-to-mechanism descent by single-element
  configuration diffs, applied to collocation contention; and a
  quality-bounded, configuration-only topology and capacity analysis of
  agentic RAG.
\end{enumerate}
